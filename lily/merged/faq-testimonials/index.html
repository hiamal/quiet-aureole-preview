Questions, answered

Hypnotherapy, IEMT and HypnoBreathWork for anxiety: frequently asked questions.

On overcoming anxiety, creating healthy boundaries, releasing guilt and shame, and how breath, the subconscious, and the nervous system work together to create lasting change.

If you’ve tried thinking your way out of anxiety — reading, reasoning, willpower — and it keeps returning, that’s usually a sign the pattern lives deeper than conscious thought. Anxiety is often the nervous system doing its job too well: protecting you based on old experiences. Hypnotherapy, breathwork and IEMT work at that subconscious and somatic level, which is why many clients notice shifts that years of “trying harder” couldn’t create.

If setting boundaries floods you with guilt, it’s rarely a communication-skills problem — it’s a safety problem. Somewhere along the way, your nervous system learned that other people’s comfort was the price of belonging. Learning how to set clear boundaries starts with releasing that old imprint, so a “no” stops feeling like a threat.

Chronic guilt — feeling responsible for everyone’s feelings, guilt when you rest, guilt when you choose yourself — is one of the most common patterns I see in women over 40. It’s usually a survival adaptation, not a moral truth. Together we explore where that imprint began and gently release it, so guilt returns to its proper role: an occasional signal, not a constant companion.

Shame is the belief that something is wrong not with what you did, but with who you are — and it tends to hide beneath anxiety, perfectionism and people pleasing. Because shame is an identity imprint, approaches like IEMT that work directly with self-concept can be particularly helpful. You don’t have to relive painful memories to release shame.

Overcoming anxiety in relationships often means noticing the survival patterns that run in connection with others — anticipating moods, avoiding conflict, abandoning your needs to keep the peace. As your nervous system settles and old imprints release, many clients find their relationships change naturally.
