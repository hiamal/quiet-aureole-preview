What is IEMT?

IEMT (Integral Eye Movement Technique) is an effective and swift enquiry model that works with emotional, memory and identity imprints. Many clients arrive frustrated — years of talking about their anxiety in counselling without the resolution they hoped for. IEMT often facilitates a very different experience, creating significant and lasting change.

Whether you carry unresolved emotions or explicit memories from past experiences, IEMT can work on these and the associated identity imprints — patterns that show up as anxiety, fears, phobias, low confidence, low mood or unwanted habits.

I often liken it to a house fire: the alarm goes off, they put out the fire — but nobody switches off the alarm. IEMT helps switch off the alarm.

How IEMT works

IEMT allows you to accurately measure and assess change in real time. Memories encoded to a particular timeframe are brought into the conscious mind and re-evaluated through the limbic system — the interconnected brain structures that help regulate emotion and behaviour — then consolidated as resolved.

Clients commonly describe a sense of space where the weight used to be — balance, calm, lightness, and mental and emotional clarity. The present and future no longer compete with the past, and the ripple effects can reach every area of life: relationships, work, finances, sleep, physical wellbeing, and the relationship with yourself.

IEMT vs EMDR

IEMT vs EMDR — what’s the difference?

It’s one of the most common questions I’m asked. Both use eye movements, but they work quite differently.

EMDR

  • Traumatic imagery is central — the client is encouraged to “observe” the trauma
  • Uses eye movements as well as tapping and alternating sounds
  • Utilises affirmations and positive belief statements

IEMT

  • Traumatic imagery is largely irrelevant to the process
  • Addresses imprints of emotion to create felt, kinaesthetic change
  • Addresses imprints of identity to create change in self-concept

For many women overcoming anxiety rooted in old emotional patterns — rather than a single traumatic event — IEMT’s gentler, imagery-light approach can feel more accessible.
