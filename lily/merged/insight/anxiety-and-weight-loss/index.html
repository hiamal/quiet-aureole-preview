Anxiety and Weight Loss

Lily’s blogMarch 01, 20263 min read

What If Your Body Is Trying to Tell You Something?

When weight becomes a struggle, the conversation often turns to food, exercise and willpower.

But weight is rarely that simple.

Our bodies are influenced by many things — nutrition, movement, sleep, stress, genetics, medications, health conditions and hormones. For women, hormonal changes such as perimenopause and menopause can also significantly influence the way the body changes.

So I don't believe every struggle with weight has an emotional cause.

But sometimes, there is an emotional layer worth exploring.

When Food Becomes About More Than Hunger

Food can provide comfort, pleasure, distraction or temporary relief.

When we are stressed, exhausted or emotionally overwhelmed, eating can sometimes become a way of changing how we feel in the moment.

The question isn't necessarily:

“Why can't I control this?”

It might be:

“What am I experiencing when I reach for food?”

And perhaps:

“What am I looking for in that moment?”

Comfort?

Relief?

Reward?

A break?

Something to help you feel different, even temporarily?

There may not always be an immediate answer.

The value is in becoming curious rather than judging yourself.

When Something Doesn't Seem to Make Sense

I worked with a client experiencing significant pressure through their business.

Initially, the demands of work appeared to be the obvious source of their struggle. But as we became curious about what was happening beneath the surface, earlier experiences began to emerge.

They had never considered those experiences traumatic. They simply remembered them as things that had happened.

Yet when they were able to observe those experiences from a different perspective, they recognised something they hadn't previously understood:

Those experiences had affected them more than they realised.

At the time, there had been little opportunity to express what they were feeling or have someone help them make sense of it.

They found ways to cope.

Over time, those ways of coping became familiar.

And sometimes, familiar can feel like normal.

Look at the Whole Picture

If you are struggling with your weight, I encourage you to look at the whole picture.

Consider your nutrition.

Your movement.

Your sleep.

Your stress levels.

Your hormones.

Your health.

And, if it resonates, your emotional relationship with food and your body.

For women experiencing changes around perimenopause or menopause in particular, hormonal factors deserve appropriate attention rather than assuming everything is emotional.

Persistent or unexplained changes in weight should always be discussed with an appropriate health professional.

My work sits alongside these considerations — not instead of them.

I don't believe you need to find an emotional wound behind every struggle.

But if you notice that anxiety, stress, emotional overwhelm or past experiences seem connected to your relationship with food or your body, that connection may be worth exploring.

You don't have to judge yourself for what you are experiencing.

You can become curious.

What is happening beneath the surface?

What might I be trying to give myself in that moment?

Sometimes, understanding that relationship can create the space for something different.

Seeing creates choice.

Choice creates freedom.
