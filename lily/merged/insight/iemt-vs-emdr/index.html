IEMT vs EMDR

Lily’s blogAugust 02, 20262 min read

Understanding the Difference

You can understand why you respond a certain way and still find yourself responding the same way.

You may know where the anxiety came from.

You may understand the relationship pattern.

You may have talked about the experience many times.

And yet the emotional response remains.

This is where IEMT may offer a different avenue for exploration.

What Is IEMT?

IEMT stands for Integral Eye Movement Technique.

It is a structured, experiential approach that explores emotional and identity patterns — how you learned to feel and how you learned to experience yourself.

Rather than repeatedly analysing the story, the focus is on what is happening now:

How do I feel?

How do I experience myself?

What response keeps appearing?

What might I have learned from this experience?

Specific eye movements and questioning are used to explore these patterns while you remain in the role of observer.

You don't necessarily need to repeatedly retell everything that happened.

IEMT vs EMDR

IEMT and EMDR are not the same therapy, although both use eye movements.

EMDR — Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing is an established psychotherapy with substantial research support, particularly for PTSD. It uses a structured treatment protocol to work with distressing memories and associated responses.

IEMT — Integral Eye Movement Technique is a different enquiry model, focusing particularly on emotional and identity imprints and how we learned to feel or experience ourselves in particular ways.

I don't see one as universally better than the other.

They are different approaches, and what is appropriate depends on the individual and what they are seeking support with.

When Knowing Why Isn't Enough

Sometimes the most frustrating experience is:

“I understand why I do this. So why do I keep doing it?”

Understanding can give us context, compassion and awareness.

But knowing something intellectually doesn't always mean our emotional response has changed.

IEMT offers an opportunity to become curious about that response rather than simply trying to suppress it or understand it further.

What happens when you observe it differently?

What remains?

What changes?

And what have you been carrying that may no longer need to be carried in the same way?

I don't promise a particular outcome, and IEMT doesn't replace appropriate medical or clinical care.

It is simply another way of exploring what may still be taking up emotional space.

Could IEMT Be Right for You?

If you've spent years understanding your story but still experience the same emotional responses, memories or patterns, perhaps there is another question worth asking:

“What do I want to be different now?”

If something here resonates, you don't need to know whether IEMT is the right approach before you reach out.

A conversation can provide space to explore what you're experiencing and whether this way of working may be appropriate for you.

Sometimes the first step isn't having the answer.

It's becoming curious about what still needs to change.
